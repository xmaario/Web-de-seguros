About me

I started UnpackInsurance because insurance is one of the most important purchases people make, yet the language in quotes and policies often makes it hard to understand what you are actually buying. My goal is simple: explain car and home insurance in plain English so you can ask better questions, compare offers fairly, and avoid surprises when you need to make a claim.

I run the site independently from Spain and publish content for readers in the United States. I am not a licensed insurance agent or broker, and I do not sell insurance. That independence is intentional: no insurer pays to appear in our articles, and nothing on this site is written to push you toward a particular company or product.

How our guides are created

  • Research first. Each guide starts from how coverage generally works in the United States, using official and widely recognized public sources.
  • Plain language. We explain terms, give simple illustrative examples, and avoid jargon wherever possible.
  • Careful wording. Insurance rules vary by state and provider, so we avoid presenting general information as if it applies everywhere, and we never promise prices.
  • No invented claims. We do not publish made-up reviews, ratings, statistics, or savings figures.
  • Corrections. If you find an error, please contact us. Reported issues are reviewed and articles are updated when needed.

Sources we rely on

When we research a topic, we prioritize official and educational sources, such as:

  • State departments of insurance and their consumer guides.
  • The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) consumer resources.
  • FEMA and the National Flood Insurance Program for flood coverage.
  • State motor vehicle agencies for licensing, SR-22, and driving record rules.
  • Federal consumer protection agencies for topics such as consumer reports and your rights.

What this site is not

UnpackInsurance provides general information, not legal, financial, tax, or insurance advice. Before buying or changing a policy, read the policy documents and speak with a licensed professional in your state. Learn more about how the site is funded on our About page.
