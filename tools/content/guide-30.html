Does Car Insurance Cover a Cracked Windshield?

A rock on the highway, a falling branch, or a hailstorm can chip or crack your windshield in a second. Whether insurance pays depends on the coverage you carry, your deductible, and whether the glass can be repaired or must be replaced. Here is how it usually works.

The short answer

Usually yes, if you have comprehensive coverage. Windshield damage from rocks, weather, falling objects, or vandalism is typically a comprehensive claim, subject to your deductible. Many insurers waive the deductible for small chip repairs. A liability-only policy generally does not cover your own windshield. Details vary by state and provider.

Which coverage applies: common situations

If your windshield was damaged by...Coverage that usually applies
A rock or debris on the roadComprehensive
Hail, a storm, or a falling branchComprehensive
Vandalism or a break-inComprehensive
A crash with another car or objectCollision, as part of the crash claim
Another driver who caused a crashThe other driver's liability coverage, or your collision
Any cause, with liability-only insuranceUsually not covered

If you are not sure which coverage you have, see Comprehensive vs. Collision Coverage.

Three common scenarios

  • If a truck kicked up a rock, it is usually hard to prove which vehicle caused it, so the claim typically goes through your own comprehensive coverage rather than the other driver's insurer.
  • If you only have a small chip, ask about a repair first; it may cost you nothing out of pocket.
  • If you carry liability-only insurance, you will likely pay for the glass yourself, so compare a few shop quotes.

Repair or replace?

Small chips and short cracks can often be repaired by filling them with resin, which is usually faster and less expensive than a replacement. Larger cracks, damage in the driver's line of sight, or damage near the edge of the glass often mean the windshield must be replaced. The repair shop or insurer will usually decide based on the size and location of the damage.

Tip

Get a small chip repaired soon. Temperature changes and road vibration can turn a chip into a long crack, which may turn a free repair into a replacement with a deductible.

How the deductible works for glass

  • Chip repairs: many insurers waive the comprehensive deductible for repairs, because an early repair can prevent a costlier replacement.
  • Replacement: your comprehensive deductible usually applies. If the replacement costs less than your deductible, a claim would pay nothing.
  • Full glass coverage: some insurers offer an add-on that lowers or removes the deductible for glass repair or replacement.
  • State rules: a few states have laws that affect windshield deductibles for drivers who carry comprehensive. Ask your insurer or your state's department of insurance.

For more on choosing deductibles, read How Car Insurance Deductibles Work.

Newer cars: camera recalibration

Many newer vehicles have cameras or sensors mounted near the windshield for features such as lane-keeping and automatic emergency braking. After a replacement, these systems may need to be recalibrated so they work correctly. Ask whether recalibration is needed, whether it is included in the claim, and whether the shop can do it.

Questions to ask before you file

  • Is this damage covered under my comprehensive coverage?
  • Will you waive the deductible for a chip repair?
  • Do I have full glass coverage, and what is my glass deductible?
  • Do I have to use a particular glass shop, or can I choose my own?
  • Will my car need camera or sensor recalibration, and is that covered?
  • Will a glass claim affect my premium or renewal?

How to file a windshield claim

  1. Take photos of the damage and note when and where it happened.
  2. Contact your insurer, by phone, online, or in the app. Some insurers handle glass claims through a dedicated glass line.
  3. Confirm the deductible and whether the damage will be repaired or replaced.
  4. Schedule the repair and keep the invoice.

For general claim steps, see How to File a Car Insurance Claim.

Watch out

Be cautious with unsolicited offers of a "free" windshield replacement, especially ones that ask for your policy details on the spot. Contact your insurer directly to start a claim.

Common questions

Will a windshield claim raise my insurance rates?

Glass claims are often treated differently from at-fault accidents, and a single small claim may not affect your premium. Practices vary by state and provider, so ask before you file.

Can I drive with a cracked windshield?

Rules vary by state, especially for cracks that block the driver's view. A large crack can also weaken the glass, so it is safest to get it inspected soon.

The bottom line

A cracked or chipped windshield is usually covered if you have comprehensive coverage. Chip repairs are often free of a deductible, while replacements usually are not, unless you have full glass coverage or live in a state with special rules. Ask your insurer the questions above before you schedule work. Coverage varies by state and provider.
