How to File a Home Insurance Claim, Step by Step

A burst pipe, a fire, a storm, or a break-in can leave you dealing with damage and stress at the same time. Knowing the usual steps of a home insurance claim can help you protect your home, avoid common mistakes, and get the claim moving sooner.

The short answer

Make sure everyone is safe, take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, and document everything with photos and video. Then contact your insurer promptly, meet with the adjuster, and submit any forms it requests. Keep receipts and records. Coverage, deadlines, and payments vary by policy, state, and provider.

Step 1: Make sure everyone is safe

If there is a fire, gas leak, flooding, or structural damage, leave the home and call emergency services. Shut off water, gas, or electricity only if it is safe to do so. If your home cannot be lived in, ask your insurer about additional living expenses, sometimes called loss of use coverage, which may help pay for temporary housing.

Step 2: Prevent further damage

Most homeowners policies require you to take reasonable steps to protect your property after a loss. This may include covering a damaged roof with a tarp, boarding up broken windows, or removing standing water. Damage that happens because you did not take reasonable steps may not be covered.

  • Make temporary repairs only, not permanent ones, until the insurer has seen the damage.
  • Keep receipts for supplies and emergency services.
  • Keep damaged items if you can, so the adjuster can inspect them.

Step 3: Document the damage

Before you clean up, take photos and video of every affected area, from wide shots to close-ups. Then start a list of damaged or missing items with a description, approximate age, and what you paid, if you know. Receipts, bank statements, and older photos of your home can help support the list.

Tip

A simple video walk-through of your home, recorded before anything goes wrong and stored online, can make a future claim much easier to document.

For a theft or vandalism claim, file a police report and keep the report number.

Step 4: Contact your insurer promptly

Most policies require prompt notice of a loss, and some set specific deadlines. You can usually start a claim by phone, online, or in an app. Have your policy number ready, describe what happened and when, and ask:

  • What is my claim number, and who is my adjuster?
  • What documents and forms do you need, and by when?
  • Is there a deadline to submit a proof of loss form?
  • Does my policy include additional living expenses?
  • Which deductible applies? Some policies have a separate wind, hail, or hurricane deductible. See Home Insurance Deductibles Explained.

Step 5: Work with the adjuster

The insurer usually sends an adjuster to inspect the damage, or asks for photos and video for smaller claims. Be present for the inspection if you can, point out all the damage, and share your photos and list. You may also ask a licensed contractor you trust for a separate estimate. Keep a log of every call and email: the date, who you spoke to, and what was agreed.

Step 6: Review the estimate and settlement

The insurer will explain what it will pay, minus your deductible. Check that every damaged area and item is included. Many policies pay in stages:

  • Actual cash value first: with replacement cost coverage, insurers often pay the depreciated value first, then pay the rest after you repair or replace. See Replacement Cost vs. Actual Cash Value.
  • Mortgage lender on the check: payments for damage to the home itself are often made out to you and your lender, which may release funds as repairs progress.
  • Additional living expenses: these may be paid separately as you incur them.

Step 7: Repair, and watch out for scams

After a storm, some contractors go door to door offering fast repairs. Get more than one estimate, check licenses and reviews from independent sources, avoid large upfront payments, and read any contract carefully. Some contractors may ask you to sign an assignment of benefits, which lets them deal with your insurer directly. Rules on these agreements vary by state, so understand what you are signing first.

Quick checklist

  • Everyone is safe and the home is secured.
  • Temporary repairs made and receipts saved.
  • Photos, video, and an itemized list of damage.
  • Police report for theft or vandalism.
  • Claim number, adjuster name, and deadlines written down.
  • A log of every call and email.

Common questions

Should I file a claim for small damage?

If the repair costs little more than your deductible, a claim may not be worth it, because some insurers consider past claims when pricing or renewing a policy. Practices vary by state and provider.

What if I disagree with the settlement?

Ask for a written explanation, share contractor estimates and other evidence, and ask whether your policy has an appraisal clause. If you cannot resolve it, you may contact your state's department of insurance.

The bottom line

Stay safe, prevent further damage, document everything, and report the claim promptly. Work closely with the adjuster, check the estimate, and keep good records.
