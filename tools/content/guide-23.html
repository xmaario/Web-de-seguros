Does Car Insurance Cover Uber and Lyft Driving? Rideshare Insurance Explained

Driving for a rideshare app can be a flexible way to earn money with your own car. But the car insurance you bought for commuting and errands may not cover you while you work. This guide explains where the gaps usually are, how coverage from the rideshare company typically works, and what you can do to stay protected.

Why your personal policy may not apply

Most personal auto policies are written for private use. Many exclude, or limit, coverage when you use your car to carry people or goods for a fee. If you have an accident while driving for a rideshare app and your policy has that kind of exclusion, your insurer may deny the claim. Some insurers may also cancel or decline to renew a policy if they learn you were driving for hire without telling them.

Policy wording varies by state and provider, so read your own policy or ask your insurer how it treats rideshare and delivery driving.

How rideshare coverage usually works by phase

Rideshare companies generally carry insurance for their drivers, and many states have laws that set minimum requirements for it. That coverage usually depends on what you are doing in the app at the time of an accident. The phases are often described like this:

  • App off: you are driving for personal reasons. Your personal policy is the one that applies, and the rideshare company's coverage generally does not.
  • App on, waiting for a request: you are available but have not accepted a ride. Rideshare company coverage in this phase is often limited, such as liability coverage with lower limits, and may not include damage to your own car. This is often the biggest gap.
  • Ride accepted, on the way to the pickup: rideshare company coverage typically increases, often with higher liability limits.
  • Passenger in the car: coverage is usually similar to the previous phase and lasts until the trip ends.

The exact limits, deductibles, and conditions depend on the company and the state, and they can change. Check the current terms in the app or on the company's website rather than relying on general descriptions.

Watch out

Coverage for damage to your own car from a rideshare company may apply only if you also carry collision and comprehensive on your personal policy, and it may come with a high deductible.

What a rideshare endorsement does

Many insurers offer a rideshare endorsement, an add-on to your personal auto policy that extends coverage to some or all of your rideshare driving. Depending on the provider, it may:

  • Cover the period when the app is on and you are waiting for a request.
  • Extend your personal collision and comprehensive to rideshare driving.
  • Help cover a deductible gap when the rideshare company's coverage applies.
  • Keep you on a personal policy rather than requiring a commercial one.

Not every insurer offers an endorsement, and availability varies by state. Some insurers offer a separate hybrid policy instead. If you drive many hours, or use your car for other business purposes, a commercial auto policy may be more appropriate.

What about food and package delivery?

Delivering food or packages raises similar questions. Personal policies may exclude it, and delivery apps may provide different, often narrower, coverage than rideshare apps. Some rideshare endorsements also cover delivery driving and some do not. Ask your insurer specifically about each type of work you do.

Steps before you start driving

  1. Read your policy. Look for exclusions related to carrying people or goods for a fee.
  2. Call your insurer. Tell them which apps you plan to use and ask how your coverage applies in each phase.
  3. Ask about an endorsement. Compare its cost with the risk of driving with gaps. If your insurer does not offer one, you may need to shop around.
  4. Review your limits. Carrying passengers may raise your exposure to injury claims. Our liability coverage calculator can help you think through limits, and How Much Car Insurance Do You Need? explains the basics.
  5. Check your own-car coverage. Rideshare company coverage for your car may depend on your personal collision and comprehensive. See What Is Full Coverage Car Insurance?
  6. Check the rideshare company's terms. Note the limits and deductibles for each phase.

If you have an accident while driving

  • Make sure everyone is safe and call emergency services if needed.
  • Report the accident in the rideshare app as soon as you can.
  • Note whether the app was on, whether you had accepted a ride, and whether a passenger was in the car.
  • Notify your own insurer too, and be honest about what you were doing.

Common questions

Do I need to tell my insurer I drive for a rideshare app?

Generally yes. Not telling your insurer may lead to a denied claim, and some insurers may cancel or not renew a policy if they find out later.

Will a rideshare endorsement raise my premium?

Usually it adds some cost, which varies by state and provider. Ask for a quote with and without it.

Does the rideshare company's insurance cover me when the app is off?

Generally no. When the app is off, your personal policy is the coverage that applies.

The bottom line

Your personal car insurance may not cover rideshare driving, and the rideshare company's coverage often depends on the phase of a trip. The time when the app is on but you have no ride is often where the biggest gap is. Talk to your insurer before you start, ask about a rideshare endorsement, and check your limits. Coverage and prices vary by state and provider.
