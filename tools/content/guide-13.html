What Is an SR-22? Who Needs One and How It Works

If a court or your state's motor vehicle agency says you need an SR-22, it can sound like a special kind of insurance. It is actually a form that proves you carry the car insurance your state requires. Here is what it means and how to handle it.

What an SR-22 is

An SR-22 is a certificate of financial responsibility. Your insurance company files it with your state's motor vehicle agency to confirm that you have at least the minimum liability coverage required by law. It is not a policy on its own. You still need an auto insurance policy; the SR-22 is attached to it as proof.

Most states use the SR-22 form. A few states use different forms or processes. For example, Florida and Virginia use a form called the FR-44 in certain cases, such as some driving-under-the-influence convictions, and it typically requires higher liability limits than the standard minimum.

Who may need an SR-22

States generally require an SR-22 after events that suggest a higher risk on the road. Common reasons include:

  • A conviction for driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
  • Driving without insurance, or being involved in an accident without insurance.
  • Serious violations such as reckless driving.
  • Accumulating too many violations or points in a short period.
  • A license suspension or revocation that you want reinstated.
  • Failing to pay a court judgment related to an accident.

The court or the motor vehicle agency will tell you if you need one and for how long. Requirements are set by each state.

How to get an SR-22

  1. Contact your insurer. Tell your current insurance company that you need an SR-22. Not all insurers offer SR-22 filings, and some may decide not to renew a policy after a serious violation.
  2. Shop if needed. If your insurer does not file SR-22s, look for one that does. Independent agents can often help drivers in this situation.
  3. Pay the filing fee. Insurers often charge a filing fee. The amount varies by state and provider.
  4. Confirm the filing. The insurer files the form with the state, often electronically. Ask for confirmation and check with the motor vehicle agency that it was received.

How long you need it

Many states require an SR-22 for about three years, but the period can be shorter or longer depending on the state and the reason. The clock may start from the date of conviction, suspension, or reinstatement, depending on state rules. Ask the motor vehicle agency for the exact end date and keep continuous coverage until then.

What happens if your coverage lapses

If your policy is canceled or lapses while an SR-22 is required, the insurer is generally required to notify the state. In many states this notice is called an SR-26. The state may then suspend your license again, and you may need to start the SR-22 period over. To avoid this, set up automatic payments and do not cancel the policy until you have confirmed the requirement has ended.

Will an SR-22 raise my premium?

The filing itself usually costs only a fee. The bigger effect on your premium comes from the event that led to the SR-22, such as a DUI conviction or driving uninsured. Insurers view these as signs of higher risk, so premiums are often significantly higher for several years. The amount varies by state, insurer, and your full driving record.

To keep costs as manageable as possible, compare quotes from several insurers that offer SR-22 filings, ask about any discounts you still qualify for, and keep your record clean from now on. Our guide on lowering your car insurance premium has more ideas.

Non-owner SR-22 policies

If you need an SR-22 but do not own a car, you may be able to buy a non-owner car insurance policy with an SR-22 filing. It typically provides liability coverage when you drive a car you do not own. This can help you meet the requirement and get your license reinstated.

Moving to another state

If you move while an SR-22 is required, you usually still need to meet the original state's requirement. Your new insurer must be able to file in the state that ordered the SR-22, and the policy should meet that state's minimum limits. Contact both states' motor vehicle agencies before you move.

Common questions

Is an SR-22 the same as insurance?

No. It is proof of insurance filed with the state. You need an active policy for the SR-22 to be valid.

Can I remove the SR-22 early?

Generally no. The requirement lasts until the state's required period ends. Ask the motor vehicle agency before making any change.

Do I need to tell my insurer when it ends?

Yes. Once the state confirms the requirement has ended, ask your insurer to stop the filing. Your premium may not drop right away, because the underlying violation may still affect your rate for some time.

The bottom line

An SR-22 is a certificate your insurer files to prove you carry required coverage. It typically lasts a few years, and letting coverage lapse can restart the process. Find an insurer that files SR-22s, keep the policy active, and focus on a clean record. Rules vary by state, so confirm the details with your state's motor vehicle agency.
