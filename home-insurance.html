Home insurance explained

Homeowners insurance is not required by law, but mortgage lenders almost always require it. A standard policy bundles several kinds of protection. Knowing what each part does can help you choose limits that fit your home and budget.

Coverage types

Dwelling coverage

Dwelling coverage helps pay to repair or rebuild the structure of your home, including the roof, walls, and built-in systems, after a covered loss such as fire or wind. The limit should reflect the cost to rebuild, which may be different from your home's market value or purchase price.

Other structures

This covers structures not attached to your house, such as a detached garage, shed, or fence. It is often set as a percentage of your dwelling limit.

Personal property

Personal property coverage helps pay to repair or replace your belongings, such as furniture, clothing, and electronics, if they are damaged or stolen. Policies may pay replacement cost (the cost to buy a new equivalent item) or actual cash value (replacement cost minus depreciation). High-value items like jewelry may have lower sub-limits unless you add extra coverage.

Personal liability

Liability coverage can help if someone is injured on your property or you accidentally damage someone else's property, and you are found legally responsible. It may pay legal defense costs and settlements up to your limit.

Loss of use (additional living expenses)

If a covered loss makes your home unlivable, loss of use coverage can help pay for temporary housing, meals, and other extra costs while repairs are made.

What standard policies often exclude

Many standard policies exclude flood and earthquake damage, as well as wear and tear, maintenance problems, and pest damage. Flood coverage is often bought separately, including through the National Flood Insurance Program, and earthquake coverage may be available as a separate policy or endorsement. Exclusions vary by state and provider, so read your policy carefully.

What affects your premium

Each insurer uses its own formula, so the same home may receive very different quotes. Factors that commonly matter include:

  • Location: exposure to hurricanes, wildfires, hail, and other weather risks, along with local crime rates and distance to a fire station.
  • Rebuild cost: the size of the home, materials, and local construction costs.
  • Age and condition: the age of the roof, plumbing, electrical, and heating systems.
  • Claims history: prior claims on the property or by the applicant may affect pricing.
  • Deductible and limits: higher limits and lower deductibles usually cost more.
  • Safety features: smoke detectors, monitored alarms, and storm-resistant construction may qualify for discounts with some providers.
  • Credit-based insurance score: used where state law allows.

Tips for choosing a policy

  1. Insure to rebuild, not to sell. Ask the provider how they estimate your rebuild cost and review it when you renovate.
  2. Make a home inventory. Photos or video of your belongings can make a claim easier and help you choose a personal property limit.
  3. Understand your deductibles. Some areas have separate percentage deductibles for wind, hail, or named storms. Read Home Insurance Deductibles Explained.
  4. Check replacement cost vs. actual cash value. This choice can make a big difference after a loss. See our guide.
  5. Consider gaps. Ask about flood, earthquake, sewer backup, and valuable-items coverage if they apply to you.
  6. Compare like for like. Request quotes from several providers, such as [CARRIER A] and [CARRIER B], using the same limits and deductibles.
