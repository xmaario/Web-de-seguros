Insurance glossary

Short, plain-English definitions of terms you will see on car and home insurance quotes and policies. Definitions are general. Your policy's own definitions are the ones that apply to you.

ACDEGLMNOPRSU

A

Actual cash value (ACV)
The cost to replace damaged property minus depreciation for age and wear. See Replacement Cost vs. Actual Cash Value.
Additional living expenses
Extra costs, such as a hotel and meals, paid under loss of use coverage when a covered loss makes your home unlivable.
Adjuster
The person who investigates a claim, inspects damage, and determines how much the insurer will pay under the policy.
Agent
A licensed professional who sells insurance. A captive agent represents one company; an independent agent can offer policies from several companies.

C

Claim
A formal request to your insurer for payment after a covered loss.
Collision coverage
Auto coverage that helps pay to repair or replace your car after it hits another vehicle or object, regardless of fault.
Comprehensive coverage
Auto coverage for non-collision damage, such as theft, hail, fire, flood, vandalism, or hitting an animal.
Credit-based insurance score
A score based on credit history that insurers in many states may use as one pricing factor. Some states restrict or ban its use.

D

Declarations page
The summary page of a policy listing who and what is covered, coverage limits, deductibles, premium, and the policy period.
Deductible
The amount you pay toward a covered loss before insurance pays. See Home Insurance Deductibles Explained.
Depreciation
The decrease in an item's value over time due to age, wear, or obsolescence.
Dwelling coverage
The part of a homeowners policy that covers the structure of the home itself.

E

Endorsement
An add-on that changes a policy, for example by adding coverage for water backup or valuable items. Also called a rider.
Exclusion
A situation, peril, or type of property that a policy specifically does not cover.
Extended replacement cost
A homeowners option that may pay a set percentage above your dwelling limit if rebuilding costs more than expected.

G

Gap coverage
Auto coverage that may pay the difference between what you owe on a loan or lease and your car's actual cash value if it is totaled.

L

Liability coverage
Coverage that pays others for injuries or property damage you are legally responsible for, up to your limit.
Limit
The maximum amount an insurer will pay for a covered loss. Auto liability limits are often written as three numbers, such as 50/100/50.
Loss of use
Home or renters coverage for additional living expenses while your home is being repaired after a covered loss.

M

Medical payments (MedPay)
Coverage that helps pay medical bills after an accident or injury, regardless of fault, usually with modest limits.

N

Named perils
Coverage that protects only against the specific events listed in the policy.
National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)
A federal program managed by FEMA that provides flood insurance. See Do You Need Flood Insurance?
No-fault insurance
A system used in some states where your own insurer pays certain injury costs through personal injury protection, regardless of who caused the accident.

O

Open perils
Coverage that protects against any cause of loss not specifically excluded. Sometimes called "all risk."

P

Peril
A cause of loss, such as fire, wind, theft, or hail.
Personal injury protection (PIP)
Auto coverage for medical costs and, in some cases, lost wages for you and your passengers, regardless of fault. Required in no-fault states.
Policyholder
The person or entity that owns the insurance policy.
Premium
The amount you pay for an insurance policy, usually monthly, every six months, or annually.

R

Replacement cost
The cost to replace damaged property with new property of similar kind and quality, without subtracting depreciation.
Rider
Another name for an endorsement.

S

Scheduled personal property
Specific valuable items, such as jewelry, listed on a policy with their own coverage amounts above standard sub-limits.
Subrogation
The process by which your insurer, after paying your claim, seeks to recover the cost from the party responsible for the loss.

U

Umbrella insurance
Extra liability coverage that applies above the limits of your home and auto policies.
Underwriting
The process insurers use to evaluate risk and decide whether to offer coverage and at what price.
Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage
Auto coverage that helps protect you if you are hit by a driver who has no insurance or not enough.

Definitions are simplified for general information. Terms and coverage vary by state and provider; your policy's definitions control.
