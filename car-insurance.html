Car insurance explained

Almost every state requires drivers to carry some form of auto insurance. Beyond the legal minimum, the right policy depends on your car, your finances, and how much risk you are comfortable taking on. This page covers the building blocks.

Coverage types

Liability coverage

Liability pays for damage you cause to other people and their property. It is usually split into bodily injury liability (medical bills, lost wages, and legal costs for people you injure) and property damage liability (repairs to other vehicles, fences, buildings, and so on). Limits are often written as three numbers, such as 50/100/50, meaning $50,000 per injured person, $100,000 per accident for injuries, and $50,000 for property damage. Most states set a minimum, but those minimums may be far lower than the cost of a serious accident.

Collision coverage

Collision helps pay to repair or replace your own car after a crash with another vehicle or an object, regardless of who is at fault. It usually comes with a deductible, which is the amount you pay before coverage applies. Lenders and leasing companies commonly require it.

Comprehensive coverage

Comprehensive covers damage to your car from events other than a collision, such as theft, vandalism, fire, falling objects, hail, flooding, or hitting an animal. Like collision, it typically has a deductible and is often required on financed or leased vehicles.

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage

If you are hit by a driver who has no insurance, or not enough, this coverage can help pay for your injuries and, in some states, damage to your car. Some states require it; others let you decline it in writing.

Medical payments and personal injury protection

Medical payments coverage (MedPay) and personal injury protection (PIP) help pay medical costs for you and your passengers after an accident, regardless of fault. PIP may also cover lost wages and other expenses. In "no-fault" states, PIP is generally required.

Optional add-ons

  • Rental reimbursement: helps pay for a rental car while yours is being repaired after a covered claim.
  • Roadside assistance: towing, jump-starts, lockout help, and flat-tire changes.
  • Gap coverage: may pay the difference between what you owe on a loan or lease and the car's actual cash value if it is totaled.

Availability, names, and terms of these options vary by state and provider.

What affects your premium

Insurers use many factors to price a policy, and each company weighs them differently. That is one reason quotes for the same driver can vary widely. Common factors include:

  • Location: your state and ZIP code affect rates because of differences in traffic, theft, weather, repair costs, and state rules.
  • Driving record: recent accidents, tickets, and violations may raise your premium.
  • Age and experience: newer and younger drivers are often charged more. Some states limit the use of certain personal factors.
  • Vehicle: the make, model, age, safety features, and repair costs of your car.
  • Annual mileage and use: driving more miles, or using the car for commuting or business, may increase risk.
  • Coverage choices: higher limits and lower deductibles generally cost more.
  • Insurance history: a gap in coverage may affect pricing.
  • Credit-based insurance score: used in many states where permitted by law; some states restrict or ban it.

Tips for comparing policies

  1. Compare identical coverage. Use the same limits, deductibles, and add-ons for every quote so price differences are meaningful.
  2. Look beyond the state minimum. Consider how much you could lose in a lawsuit and whether higher liability limits make sense for you.
  3. Match your deductible to your savings. A higher deductible may lower your premium, but only choose one you could pay on short notice.
  4. Ask about discounts. Providers may offer discounts for things like safe driving, bundling policies, or vehicle safety features. Eligibility varies.
  5. Check how claims work. Ask how to file a claim, whether you can choose a repair shop, and what support is available.
  6. Review every year. Your needs change when you move, buy a car, or add a driver. Recheck your coverage at renewal.
