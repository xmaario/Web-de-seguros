How to Lower Your Car Insurance Premium

Published November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Car insurance is a recurring cost, so even a modest reduction adds up over the years. The goal is to pay less without leaving yourself exposed to a loss you could not afford. These steps focus on changes that are within your control.

Key takeaways

  • Compare quotes at least once a year with identical coverage.
  • Raise deductibles only to amounts you could pay from savings.
  • Ask about every discount you may qualify for.
  • Avoid cutting liability coverage just to save money.

1. Compare quotes regularly

Insurers use different rating formulas, so the same driver can receive very different prices from different companies. Comparing quotes at least once a year, and whenever your situation changes, is often the most effective way to find a better price. Make sure every quote uses the same limits and deductibles so you are comparing like for like. Our guide on comparing car insurance quotes walks through the process.

2. Review your deductibles

A higher collision and comprehensive deductible usually lowers your premium because you agree to pay more of each claim yourself. Only choose a deductible you could pay quickly from savings. If raising it saves very little, it may not be worth the extra risk. Learn more in How Car Insurance Deductibles Work.

3. Reconsider coverage on an older car

Collision and comprehensive pay up to your car's actual cash value, minus the deductible. As a car ages and loses value, the coverage may cost more than it is worth to you. If the car is paid off and you could replace it from savings, dropping one or both coverages may make sense. Read What Is Full Coverage Car Insurance? before you decide.

Be careful about cutting liability coverage to save money. Liability protects your savings and income if you cause a serious accident, and low limits can leave you personally responsible for the difference.

4. Ask about every discount

Insurers offer a wide range of discounts, and they are not always applied automatically. Depending on the provider and your state, you may be eligible for discounts for:

  • A clean driving record or several years without claims.
  • Bundling auto with home, renters, or condo insurance.
  • Insuring more than one vehicle on the same policy.
  • Vehicle safety and anti-theft features.
  • Completing an approved defensive driving course.
  • Good grades for students, or a student living away at school without a car.
  • Paying in full, enrolling in automatic payments, or going paperless.
  • Membership in certain professional groups, alumni associations, or employers.

Ask each provider to list the discounts you qualify for and confirm they appear on your quote.

5. Consider a usage-based program

Many insurers offer programs that track driving through a mobile app or a device in the car. They may measure mileage, hard braking, speed, and the time of day you drive. Safe, low-mileage drivers may earn a discount. In some programs, risky driving can raise your rate, so read the terms carefully before you enroll and understand what data is collected.

6. Update your mileage and how you use the car

If you now work from home, commute less, or use your car mainly for errands, tell your insurer. Lower annual mileage may reduce your premium. Make sure the information is accurate, though. Understating mileage could cause problems if you file a claim.

7. Protect your driving record

Tickets and at-fault accidents are among the biggest drivers of higher premiums, and they can affect your rate for several years. Safe driving is the most reliable long-term way to keep costs down. If you receive a ticket, ask whether your state allows a traffic school option that keeps the violation off your record.

8. Think about claims before you file them

For a small loss that is only slightly more than your deductible, paying out of pocket may be cheaper over time than filing a claim, because some insurers consider claim history at renewal. Always report accidents involving injuries or damage to other people's property, as your policy requires.

9. Choose your next car with insurance in mind

The make and model of your car affects your premium. Vehicles with high repair costs, high theft rates, or powerful engines often cost more to insure. Before you buy, ask for quotes on the specific vehicles you are considering.

10. Keep continuous coverage

A gap in coverage, even a short one, may lead to higher prices when you buy a new policy. If you are selling a car and not replacing it right away, ask about a non-owner policy or other options to avoid a lapse.

11. Mind your credit where it is used

In states where it is allowed, insurers may use a credit-based insurance score as one rating factor. Paying bills on time and keeping credit balances manageable may help over time. Several states restrict or prohibit this practice, so its effect depends on where you live.

12. Review your policy at every renewal

Your needs change. A teen may move out, you may pay off a loan, or a discount may expire. Read your renewal notice, check that drivers, vehicles, and addresses are correct, and ask questions about any increase. See Why Did My Car Insurance Go Up? for common reasons.

What not to do

  • Do not let coverage lapse to save money for a month or two.
  • Do not give inaccurate information, such as listing the wrong primary driver or garaging address. It can lead to a denied claim or a canceled policy.
  • Do not drop to the legal minimum without thinking it through. Read How Much Car Insurance Do You Really Need? first.

The bottom line

The best savings usually come from comparing quotes, choosing sensible deductibles, collecting every discount you qualify for, and keeping a clean record. Each change should be weighed against the protection you give up. Prices vary by state and provider, so check the real effect of each change with a quote.
