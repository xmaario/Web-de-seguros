Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Roof Damage?

Published November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Your roof is one of the most exposed and expensive parts of your home. Whether homeowners insurance pays for roof damage depends mainly on what caused it, how old the roof is, and how your policy values it.

Key takeaways

  • Sudden damage from wind, hail, or falling trees is often covered.
  • Wear and tear, age, and poor maintenance usually are not.
  • Older roofs may be paid at actual cash value or on a payment schedule.
  • Separate wind and hail deductibles can be large.

The short answer

Homeowners insurance generally covers roof damage caused by sudden, accidental events listed in or not excluded by your policy, such as wind, hail, fire, or a tree falling on the house. It generally does not cover damage from age, wear and tear, poor maintenance, or faulty installation. The details depend on your policy and state.

Causes that are often covered

  • Wind: shingles torn off or lifted during a storm.
  • Hail: punctures, cracks, or granule loss from hailstones. Some policies limit coverage for purely cosmetic hail damage; see below.
  • Falling objects: trees or large branches that fall on the roof.
  • Fire and lightning.
  • Weight of ice, snow, or sleet under many policies.

If a covered event damages the roof and water then enters the home, the resulting interior damage is often covered as well. If water enters through an opening created by wear and tear, the interior damage may not be.

Causes that are usually not covered

  • Age and wear: shingles that curl, crack, or fail simply because the roof is old.
  • Lack of maintenance: clogged gutters, ignored leaks, or moss and rot over time.
  • Poor installation or design defects.
  • Pests: damage from animals or insects, in many policies.
  • Gradual leaks that went unrepaired.
  • Flood and earthquake, which are typically excluded and require separate coverage.

How roof age affects your payout

Policies handle roofs in different ways, and this can make a large difference after a loss:

  • Replacement cost: pays to repair or replace the roof with similar materials without subtracting for age, minus your deductible.
  • Actual cash value: subtracts depreciation, so an older roof may receive a much smaller payment.
  • Roof payment schedules: some policies pay a percentage of the cost based on the roof's age and material.

Some insurers apply actual cash value to roofs over a certain age even when the rest of the home has replacement cost coverage. Read Replacement Cost vs. Actual Cash Value for more detail, and check your declarations page or endorsements for any roof-specific terms.

Cosmetic damage exclusions

In hail-prone areas, some policies exclude cosmetic damage, such as dents on metal roofing that do not affect how the roof keeps out water. If your policy has this exclusion, you may receive nothing for dents that only affect appearance. Ask your insurer whether such an exclusion applies.

Wind and hail deductibles

Many homeowners in storm-prone regions have a separate wind or hail deductible, often a percentage of the dwelling coverage limit. A 2 percent deductible on a $300,000 dwelling limit means $6,000 out of pocket before coverage applies. Our guide to home insurance deductibles explains how these work.

How to file a roof claim

  1. Stay safe. Do not climb onto a damaged roof. Use binoculars or photos from the ground.
  2. Prevent further damage. Policies usually require reasonable steps to protect the home, such as covering a hole with a tarp. Keep receipts for emergency repairs.
  3. Document everything. Photograph the roof, gutters, siding, and any interior water damage. Note the date and the storm.
  4. Contact your insurer promptly. Many policies set deadlines for reporting storm damage.
  5. Meet the adjuster. You may also have a trusted roofer inspect the roof and attend.
  6. Review the estimate. Ask how depreciation, your deductible, and any roof schedule were applied.

Beware of storm chasers

After major storms, some contractors go door to door offering free roofs or promising to cover your deductible. Be cautious. Choose licensed, local contractors with verifiable references, read contracts carefully before signing, and be wary of anyone who asks you to sign over your insurance benefits. Some states have laws limiting these practices, and waiving a deductible can be illegal in certain states.

How to protect your roof and your coverage

  • Have the roof inspected periodically and after major storms.
  • Keep gutters clean and trim overhanging branches.
  • Keep records of roof installation and repairs, including dates and materials.
  • Ask whether impact-resistant roofing may qualify for a discount with your insurer.

Common questions

Will my insurer replace the whole roof or just repair it?

Insurers generally pay to restore the damaged area to its condition before the loss. Whether that means a repair or a full replacement depends on the extent of the damage, the materials, and your policy terms. If matching materials are no longer available, ask how your policy and state rules handle mismatched repairs.

The bottom line

Sudden damage from wind, hail, or a falling tree is often covered, while wear and tear is not. Your roof's age, your policy's valuation method, and any separate wind or hail deductible can greatly affect what you receive. Review your policy before storm season and act quickly after damage.
