Minimum Car Insurance Requirements by State

Published November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Every state except New Hampshire requires drivers to carry some car insurance or prove they can pay for damage they cause. The minimum is usually shown as three numbers, such as 25/50/25. Below is the minimum in every state and DC, the other coverages some states require, and why the legal minimum is rarely enough on its own.

Key takeaways

  • Almost every state requires liability insurance, usually written as three numbers like 25/50/25.
  • Several states raised their minimums in 2025 and 2026, including California, Virginia, North Carolina, New Jersey and Hawaii.
  • Some states also require PIP or uninsured motorist coverage.
  • The legal minimum is a floor, not a recommendation: a serious crash can cost far more.

The short answer

Most states require liability coverage of about 25/50/25: $25,000 for one injured person, $50,000 for all injuries in one accident, and $25,000 for property damage. Some states require more, such as Alaska, Maine, North Carolina and Virginia, and some also require personal injury protection (PIP) or uninsured motorist coverage. These are legal floors, not recommended amounts.

How to read the numbers

In 25/50/25, the first number is the most your policy pays for injuries to one person, the second is the most for all injuries in one accident, and the third is the most for damage to other people's property, such as their car. All three are in thousands of dollars. For a full explanation, read Liability Limits Explained.

Notes:

  • Florida: requires $10,000 of PIP and $10,000 of property damage liability. Bodily injury liability is generally not required unless you have certain accidents or violations.
  • Michigan: 50/100/10 is the lowest liability you can choose. Policies default to higher limits unless you pick lower ones in writing, and Michigan also requires PIP and $1 million of property protection insurance for damage in the state.
  • New Hampshire: insurance is not mandatory, but drivers must be able to pay for damage they cause. If you buy a policy, it must meet 25/50/25 plus medical payments and uninsured motorist coverage.

  • Some states let you meet the requirement with a combined single limit instead of three separate limits.

States that raised their minimums recently

  • California: 15/30/5 to 30/60/15 on January 1, 2025.
  • Utah: 25/65/15 to 30/65/25 on January 1, 2025.
  • Virginia: 30/60/20 to 50/100/25 for policies issued or renewed from January 1, 2025.
  • Massachusetts: 20/40/5 to 25/50/30 on July 1, 2025.
  • North Carolina: 30/60/25 to 50/100/50 for policies issued or renewed from July 1, 2025.
  • New Jersey and Hawaii: higher minimums in 2026 (35/70/25 and 40/80/20).

If you live in one of these states, check that your policy shows the new limits after your next renewal.

Why the minimum is often not enough

Liability insurance pays for harm you cause to others, up to your limits. If a crash costs more, you can be personally responsible for the rest. A single hospital stay or a new car can cost more than $25,000, so state minimums can run out quickly in a serious accident.

Example

You cause a crash and the other driver's medical bills reach $60,000. With 25/50/25, your insurer pays up to $25,000 for that person. The other $35,000 could become your responsibility. These numbers are illustrative only.

That is why many people choose limits such as 100/300/100, especially if they own a home or have savings. Our liability insurance calculator gives you a starting point, and How Much Car Insurance Do You Need? walks through the decision. If you have significant assets, an umbrella policy can add another layer.

What the minimum does not cover

  • Your own car: liability never pays to repair your car. That is what collision and comprehensive coverage do. See Comprehensive vs. Collision Coverage.
  • Your own injuries, unless your state requires PIP or you add medical payments coverage.
  • A loan or lease: lenders usually require collision and comprehensive on top of the state minimum.

Common questions

Which state has the lowest minimum?

Among states that require bodily injury liability, Pennsylvania (15/30/5) and Louisiana (15/30/25) are among the lowest. Florida does not generally require bodily injury liability at all.

Which state has the highest minimum?

North Carolina (50/100/50), Alaska, Maine and Virginia (50/100/25) are among the highest.

What happens if I drive without the minimum?

Penalties vary by state and can include fines, license or registration suspension, and having to file an SR-22. See What Is an SR-22?

The bottom line

The state minimum is the least you can legally carry, not what protects you best. Find your state in the table, check that your policy meets the current rules, and then decide whether higher limits make sense for what you own. Requirements change, so confirm with your state insurance department before you buy.
