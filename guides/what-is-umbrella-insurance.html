What Is Umbrella Insurance and Do You Need It?

Published by Mario González Ordóñez · Updated November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Your home and auto policies each have liability limits. A serious accident or lawsuit can exceed those limits, leaving your savings and future income exposed. Umbrella insurance is designed to provide an extra layer of liability protection on top of them.

How umbrella insurance works

An umbrella policy is excess liability coverage. It generally applies after your underlying policies, such as auto, homeowners, renters, or condo insurance, have paid up to their liability limits. For example, if you are found responsible for damages that exceed your auto policy's bodily injury limit, an umbrella policy may cover the remaining amount, up to the umbrella limit.

Umbrella policies are commonly sold in increments of $1 million. They usually also help pay legal defense costs, which can be substantial even when a claim against you is not successful.

What umbrella insurance may cover

  • Bodily injury liability: injuries to others for which you are responsible, such as in a car accident or a fall at your home.
  • Property damage liability: damage you cause to someone else's property.
  • Certain personal liability claims: many policies include claims such as libel, slander, or defamation, which your home policy may not cover.
  • Claims in other places: many umbrella policies apply in more situations and locations than your underlying policies.
  • Legal defense costs.

Coverage details vary by provider, so read the policy's insuring agreement and exclusions carefully.

What it usually does not cover

  • Your own injuries or property: umbrella insurance is liability coverage, not protection for your car or home.
  • Business activities: generally excluded; businesses need commercial coverage.
  • Intentional or criminal acts.
  • Contractual liability in many policies.
  • Certain vehicles or activities, which may be excluded or require disclosure, such as some recreational vehicles or watercraft.

Underlying limits

Insurers usually require you to carry minimum liability limits on your underlying policies before they will sell you an umbrella policy. For example, they may require auto liability limits above the state minimum and a certain amount of personal liability on your homeowners policy. If a claim falls into a gap because your underlying limits are lower than required, you may have to pay that difference yourself. Ask the insurer for the exact underlying limits it requires and adjust your policies to match.

Who may want umbrella coverage

Umbrella insurance is not just for wealthy households. You may want to consider it if:

  • You own a home or have meaningful savings or investments.
  • You have a significant income that could be pursued in a lawsuit.
  • You have a teen driver in the household.
  • You have a pool, trampoline, or dog, or you often host guests.
  • You rent out property or volunteer on a board.
  • You have a public presence online, where defamation claims could arise.

How much coverage to buy

A common starting point is to choose a limit at least equal to your net worth, then consider your future earnings and the risks in your household. Because umbrella policies often cost relatively little for the amount of coverage they provide, compared with raising limits on individual policies, many people find additional coverage worthwhile. The cost varies by state, provider, and household risk, so ask for quotes at different limits.

Where to buy it

Many insurers sell umbrella policies, and some require you to carry your home and auto policies with them. Buying from the same company can simplify claims and may qualify for bundling discounts. Independent agents can compare options from several insurers.

Questions to ask

  • What underlying limits do I need on my auto and home policies?
  • Does the policy include defense costs in addition to the limit?
  • Are there exclusions for my vehicles, watercraft, pets, or rental properties?
  • Does it cover claims such as defamation?
  • Does it cover everyone in my household, including teen drivers?

Common questions

Does umbrella insurance cover me if someone is hurt by my dog?

Often yes, once your homeowners or renters liability limit is used up, but some policies exclude certain breeds or animals with a bite history. Disclose your pets when you apply.

Does it cover other drivers in my household?

Many umbrella policies cover resident relatives, including licensed teen drivers, as long as their vehicles meet the required underlying auto limits. Confirm who is covered before you buy.

Can I buy an umbrella policy if I rent?

Yes. Renters can usually buy umbrella coverage if they carry renters insurance with the required liability limit.

The bottom line

Umbrella insurance adds liability protection above your home and auto policies and can protect your savings and future income from large claims. Check the required underlying limits, review exclusions, and choose a limit that reflects what you have to protect. Coverage and prices vary by state and provider.
