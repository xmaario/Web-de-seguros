How Long Does a Car Insurance Claim Take?

Published November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

After an accident, you probably want your car fixed and your bills paid as soon as possible. How long that takes depends on the type of claim, who was at fault, and how quickly everyone involved responds. This guide explains the usual stages, what tends to slow things down, and what you can do to keep a claim moving.

Key takeaways

  • Simple claims with clear fault and no injuries are often the fastest to settle.
  • Injuries, disputed fault, and total losses usually add time.
  • Many states set deadlines for insurers to acknowledge and respond to claims.
  • Quick, complete answers and good records can help keep your claim moving.

The short answer

There is no single timeline. A simple claim with clear fault and no injuries, such as a cracked windshield or a minor repair, may be settled within days or a few weeks. Claims involving injuries, disputed fault, a total loss, or several insurers often take longer, sometimes months. Timelines vary by state and provider.

The usual stages of a claim

  1. Reporting the claim. You notify your insurer, or the other driver's insurer, by phone, website, or app.
  2. Acknowledgment. The insurer confirms it received the claim and usually assigns an adjuster.
  3. Investigation. The adjuster reviews the facts, which may include statements, photos, police reports, and witness information, to decide what is covered and who is at fault.
  4. Damage appraisal. The car is inspected in person, through photos you submit, or at a repair shop, and a repair estimate is prepared.
  5. Settlement offer. The insurer explains what it will pay, minus any deductible that applies.
  6. Payment and repairs. The insurer pays you, the repair shop, or your lender. Repairs then follow, and the shop may find more damage and ask for a supplement.

Typical timelines by claim type

These ranges are general and only meant as a rough guide. Your own claim may be faster or slower.

Claim typeOften takesWhat can add time
Glass or minor damageDays to a couple of weeksParts availability, scheduling
Repairs with clear faultA few weeksShop backlogs, supplements
Disputed faultSeveral weeks or moreStatements, police reports, other insurers
Total lossSeveral weeks or moreValuation questions, loan payoff, title paperwork
Injury claimsMonths or longerOngoing treatment, medical records, negotiations

What can slow a claim down

  • Disputed fault: when drivers tell different stories, the investigation takes longer.
  • Injuries: injury claims often stay open until treatment ends and medical costs are known.
  • Several insurers: claims involving more than one insurer may need extra coordination.
  • Missing information: delays in sending photos, documents, or statements can pause the process.
  • Total loss paperwork: if the car is totaled, the insurer may need the title and a payoff from your lender. See What Is Gap Insurance? if you owe more than the car is worth.
  • Repair shop delays: backlogs or parts shortages can stretch repairs even after the claim is approved.
  • Coverage questions: if it is unclear whether the policy covers the loss, the review may take longer.

Do insurers have deadlines?

Many states have rules, often called fair claims practices rules, that set time limits for insurers to acknowledge a claim, respond to communications, and accept or deny a claim after it has the information it needs. The details vary by state, and insurers can usually ask for more time if they have a reasonable need. Your state's department of insurance can explain the rules that apply to you.

Tip

Keep a simple log of every call and email about your claim: the date, who you spoke to, and what was agreed. It helps if you need to follow up or file a complaint.

How to help your claim move faster

  • Report the claim as soon as you reasonably can. See How to File a Car Insurance Claim for the steps.
  • Send photos, the police report number, and other documents promptly.
  • Answer calls and emails from your adjuster quickly and keep your contact details current.
  • Ask what the next step is and when you should expect to hear back.
  • Check whether your policy includes rental reimbursement, which may help while your car is being repaired.
  • If another driver was at fault, ask whether filing under your own collision coverage may be faster. See Comprehensive vs. Collision Coverage.

If your claim seems stuck

  1. Contact your adjuster in writing, ask for a status update, and ask what is still needed.
  2. If you get no answer, ask to speak with a supervisor or claims manager.
  3. Review your policy and any letters you received for deadlines that apply to you.
  4. If you still cannot get a response, you may file a complaint with your state's department of insurance.
  5. For large or complex injury claims, some people talk to an attorney.

Common questions

Does filing a claim affect my premium?

It may, especially for at-fault accidents. Read How Long Does an Accident Stay on Your Insurance?

Can I get my car repaired before the claim is settled?

Usually it is best to wait for the insurer's approval or inspection first, unless repairs are needed to prevent further damage. Ask your adjuster before you authorize work.

The bottom line

Simple claims are often settled quickly, while injuries, disputed fault, and total losses usually take longer. Report promptly, respond quickly, and keep good records. If a claim stalls, follow up in writing and contact your state's department of insurance if needed. Timelines and rules vary by state and provider.
