How to File a Car Insurance Claim, Step by Step

Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

An accident is stressful, and the claims process can feel confusing when you are already shaken. Knowing the steps ahead of time can help you protect yourself, document what happened, and get your claim moving.

Key takeaways

  • Check for injuries and call 911 if anyone is hurt.
  • Exchange information and document the scene with photos.
  • Report the claim promptly and keep records of every conversation.
  • If you disagree with a settlement, ask for details in writing and escalate if needed.

The short answer

To file a car insurance claim, report the accident to your insurer as soon as you can, give them the facts and your documentation, and work with the adjuster they assign. In order:

  1. Make sure everyone is safe and call 911 if needed.
  2. Call the police when required or useful.
  3. Exchange information with the other driver.
  4. Photograph the scene and the damage.
  5. Report the claim to your insurer.
  6. Work with the adjuster on the inspection and estimate.
  7. Get repairs, or a settlement if the car is a total loss.
  8. Appeal or ask for a review if you disagree.

Step 1: Make sure everyone is safe

Your first priority is safety. Check whether anyone is hurt and call 911 if there are injuries or if the vehicles are blocking traffic in a dangerous spot. If it is safe and your state allows it, move the vehicles out of traffic and turn on your hazard lights. Do not leave the scene of an accident before exchanging information, as that may be illegal.

Step 2: Contact the police when appropriate

Many states require you to report accidents that involve injuries or property damage above a certain amount. Even when it is not required, a police report can be useful evidence for your claim. If officers come to the scene, ask how to get a copy of the report and write down the report number.

Step 3: Exchange information

Collect the following from every other driver involved:

  • Full name and contact information.
  • Insurance company name and policy number.
  • Driver's license number and license plate number.
  • Vehicle make, model, and color.

Also get names and phone numbers of any witnesses. Stay calm and stick to the facts. Avoid admitting fault or guessing about what caused the crash, because fault is determined later based on the evidence and your state's laws.

Step 4: Document the scene

Use your phone to take photos and video of all vehicles from several angles, the damage up close, license plates, road conditions, traffic signs, skid marks, and any injuries. Write down the date, time, location, weather, and a short description of what happened while it is fresh in your memory. These details can make a real difference if there is a disagreement later.

Step 5: Report the claim to your insurer

Contact your insurance company as soon as you reasonably can. Most policies require prompt notice, and delays can complicate the process. Many insurers let you start a claim by phone, website, or app. Have your policy number, the information you collected, and your photos ready.

You may also be able to file a claim directly with the other driver's insurer if they were at fault. This is called a third-party claim. Filing with your own insurer, called a first-party claim, may move faster, especially if you have collision coverage, but you may have to pay your deductible upfront. If the other driver is found at fault, your insurer may try to recover the costs, and possibly your deductible, from their insurer through a process called subrogation.

Step 6: Work with the adjuster

An insurance adjuster will be assigned to review your claim. The adjuster may inspect your car in person, ask for photos, or direct you to an approved repair shop or drive-in inspection site. They will estimate the damage and review coverage under your policy.

Tips for working with the adjuster:

  • Answer questions honestly and keep your descriptions factual.
  • Keep a log of every call and email, including dates, names, and what was discussed.
  • Ask what your policy covers for this claim, including rental reimbursement and towing.
  • Save all receipts for related expenses, such as towing or a rental car.

Step 7: Get repairs or a total loss settlement

If the car can be repaired, you will typically receive an estimate. In many states, you have the right to choose your own repair shop, although the insurer may offer a network shop with additional benefits. If the shop finds additional damage during repairs, it can usually submit a supplement to the insurer.

If repair costs approach or exceed the car's value, the insurer may declare it a total loss. In that case, the insurer generally pays the car's actual cash value, minus your deductible. If you have a loan, the payment may go to your lender first. If you owe more than the car is worth, gap coverage, if you have it, may help cover the difference.

Step 8: If you disagree with the decision

If you believe a settlement offer is too low or a claim was denied unfairly, you have options:

  1. Ask for an explanation in writing, including how the value or estimate was calculated.
  2. Provide your own evidence, such as independent repair estimates or listings for comparable vehicles.
  3. Check your policy for an appraisal clause, which some policies include for disputes about the amount of a loss.
  4. Contact your state's department of insurance if you cannot resolve the issue. Most states accept consumer complaints about insurers.
  5. Consider professional advice from an attorney for claims involving serious injuries or significant amounts.
Watch out

Stick to facts when you describe the accident to anyone, including the other driver's insurer. Avoid guessing about speed, fault, or injuries, and do not sign anything you do not understand.

Should you file a claim for minor damage?

If the damage is only slightly more than your deductible, you might decide to pay for repairs yourself. Some insurers consider claim history when setting future rates, and practices vary by state and provider. However, if another person was injured or their property was damaged, you should notify your insurer even if you plan to pay, because a claim against you could arise later. Check your policy's notice requirements.

A quick checklist

  • Check for injuries and call 911 if needed.
  • Move to safety and turn on hazard lights.
  • Exchange information and collect witness details.
  • Take photos and write down what happened.
  • Report the claim promptly.
  • Keep records of every conversation and expense.

Common questions

How long does a car insurance claim take?

Simple claims with clear fault may be settled within a few weeks. Claims involving injuries, disputed fault, or a total loss often take longer. Many states set deadlines for insurers to acknowledge and respond to claims; your state's department of insurance can tell you the rules. For a fuller breakdown, see how long a car insurance claim takes.

How do I file an insurance claim if the other driver was at fault?

You can usually file with the other driver's insurer, or with your own if you have the right coverage. Using your own collision coverage may be faster, but you may pay your deductible first and wait for your insurer to recover it.

Can I file a claim online?

Most insurers let you start a claim online or in their app, by phone, or through your agent. Have your policy number, photos, and the other driver's information ready.

The bottom line

A smooth claim starts at the scene: stay safe, collect information, and document everything. Report the claim promptly, keep clear records, and ask questions whenever something is unclear. Keeping a copy of this checklist in your glove box or phone can make a stressful day a little easier.
