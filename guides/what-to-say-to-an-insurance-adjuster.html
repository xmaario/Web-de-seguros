What to Say (and Not Say) to an Insurance Adjuster

Published November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

After a car accident, an insurance adjuster will usually contact you to ask what happened. What you say can shape how the claim is handled, so it helps to be prepared. This guide explains who adjusters are, what to share, what to avoid, and how to keep a clear record.

Key takeaways

  • Stick to facts you know firsthand and avoid guessing or speculating.
  • Avoid statements that sound like admitting fault; fault is decided by the facts.
  • Know whether you are talking to your own insurer's adjuster or the other driver's.
  • Take notes, ask for things in writing, and do not rush to settle injury claims.

The short answer

Be polite, honest, and brief. Share facts you know firsthand, such as the date, location, and what you saw, and provide your documents. Avoid guessing, apologizing in ways that sound like admitting fault, or describing injuries before you have been checked. Take notes on every conversation and ask for important things in writing.

Who the adjuster works for

An adjuster investigates a claim and estimates what the insurer should pay. There are usually two kinds you may hear from:

  • Your own insurer's adjuster: your policy usually requires you to cooperate with your insurer, which may include answering questions and giving a statement.
  • The other driver's insurer's adjuster: this adjuster represents the other driver's insurer, not you. You may not be required to give them a recorded statement, though rules and practices vary by state.

Some people also hire a public adjuster or an attorney to represent them, often for large or complex claims.

"I'm fine, no injuries.""I'm still being checked by a doctor."
"I think they were going about 50.""I don't know their exact speed."
"I guess I could have braked sooner."Stick to what you saw and did.
"That offer sounds fine.""I'd like that in writing so I can review it."

Information you should share

  • Your name, contact details, and policy number.
  • The date, time, and location of the accident.
  • The other driver's name, insurer, and vehicle details.
  • The police report number, if there is one.
  • Photos of the scene and the damage, and witness contact details.
  • A short, factual description of what happened.

Things to avoid

  • Admitting fault or blaming yourself. Fault is usually decided from the facts, the police report, and state law, not from a casual comment.
  • Guessing. If you do not know something, such as speeds or distances, say so.
  • Downplaying injuries. Some injuries show up later. Get checked before you describe your condition.
  • Signing broad forms quickly. Read any medical authorization or release carefully. A release may end your right to claim more later.
  • Accepting the first offer on an injury claim before you know the full extent of your injuries and costs.
  • Posting about the accident on social media, which may be reviewed during a claim.
Watch out

If the other driver's insurer asks for a recorded statement, you can usually ask what it will cover and take time to prepare, or decline and provide information in writing. Check with your own insurer first.

How to prepare for the call

  1. Gather your documents before the call: policy, photos, police report number, and repair estimates.
  2. Write a short timeline of what happened, in order.
  3. Take notes during the call: the date, the adjuster's name and contact details, the claim number, and what was agreed.
  4. Ask questions: what happens next, what they still need, and when you should hear back.
  5. Follow up in writing with a short email summarizing important points.

For the full claim process, see How to File a Car Insurance Claim, and for timelines, read How Long Does a Car Insurance Claim Take?

If you disagree with the adjuster

  • Ask for a written explanation of the decision or the estimate.
  • Provide additional evidence, such as repair estimates, photos, or medical records.
  • Ask to speak with a supervisor if the issue is not resolved.
  • For a disputed car value after a total loss, see What Happens When Your Car Is Totaled?
  • If you still cannot resolve it, you may contact your state's department of insurance or talk to an attorney.

Common questions

Do I have to talk to the other driver's insurance adjuster?

You may need to provide basic information, but you are often not required to give the other insurer a recorded statement. Your own insurer can usually handle communication with them. Rules vary by state.

Should I give a recorded statement to my own insurer?

Your policy usually requires you to cooperate with your own insurer, and that may include a recorded statement. It is reasonable to ask what the statement will cover, review your notes and photos first, and answer only what you know. If you do not remember something, it is fine to say so rather than guess.

Can I say sorry after an accident?

Being polite is fine, but avoid wording that sounds like you are accepting blame. Stick to the facts.

The bottom line

Be honest, factual, and brief with adjusters. Share what you know, avoid guessing or admitting fault, and do not rush to settle injury claims. Keep notes and get important things in writing. Rules and practices vary by state and provider.
