Car insurance questions, answered

Short answers to the car insurance questions people ask most, based on what readers search for. Each answer links to a full guide if you want the details. Answers are general and rules vary by state and provider.

Jump to: Basics · Coverage · Deductibles · Costs and saving money · Claims and accidents · Car loans and gap insurance · Drivers and special situations

Basics

Is car insurance required?

In nearly every state, yes. Almost all states require liability insurance, and some also require personal injury protection (PIP) or uninsured motorist coverage. New Hampshire is the main exception, although drivers there must still be able to pay for damage they cause. Read more: minimum car insurance by state.

What does 25/50/25 mean?

It is a liability limit: up to $25,000 for injuries to one person, $50,000 for all injuries in one accident, and $25,000 for damage to other people's property. Your insurer pays up to those amounts for harm you cause; anything above them can become your responsibility. Read more: liability limits explained.

What is full coverage car insurance?

"Full coverage" is not an official policy type. It usually means liability plus collision and comprehensive, which together cover harm you cause to others and damage to your own car. Lenders usually require it on a financed or leased car. Read more: what full coverage means.

What is a car insurance quote?

A quote is an insurer's estimate of what a policy would cost, based on the details you give: drivers, car, location, coverages and deductibles. It is not final until the insurer checks your information and issues the policy. Read more: how to compare quotes.

Coverage

What is the difference between comprehensive and collision?

Collision pays to repair your car after a crash with another car or an object. Comprehensive pays for damage from things other than a crash, such as theft, hail, fire, flooding, falling objects or hitting an animal. Read more: comprehensive vs. collision.

Do I need collision insurance?

It is usually required if your car is financed or leased. If you own an older car outright, collision may cost more over time than the car is worth, so some people drop it once they could replace the car from savings. Read more: when collision makes sense.

When should I drop collision and comprehensive?

A common rule of thumb is to consider it when the yearly cost of these coverages approaches 10% of the car's value, or when you could easily replace the car yourself. If you still owe money on the car, your lender will usually require them. Read more: full coverage.

How much car insurance do I need?

At least your state minimum, but many people choose more. A common approach is liability limits high enough to protect your savings and home, plus collision and comprehensive if you could not easily replace your car. Read more: how much car insurance you need, liability insurance calculator.

Is the state minimum enough?

Often not. Minimum limits can run out quickly after a serious accident, and you may be personally responsible for costs above your limits. The minimum is a legal floor, not a recommendation. Read more: state minimums.

What is uninsured motorist coverage?

It pays for your injuries, and in some states your car, when the at-fault driver has no insurance. Underinsured motorist coverage helps when the other driver's limits are too low to cover your costs. Some states require one or both.

Does car insurance cover a cracked windshield?

Usually under comprehensive coverage, if you carry it. Many insurers waive the deductible for a small chip repair, and some policies include full glass coverage. Liability-only policies do not cover your own glass. Read more: does car insurance cover a cracked windshield.

Does car insurance cover rideshare driving?

Personal policies often exclude driving for Uber or Lyft. The rideshare company's coverage depends on whether the app is on and whether you have a passenger, and a rideshare endorsement can fill the gaps in between. Read more: rideshare insurance explained.

Does rideshare insurance cover my passengers?

Generally yes, during a trip. From the moment you accept a ride until the passenger gets out, the rideshare company's commercial policy usually provides liability coverage that can pay for passengers' injuries if you cause a crash. Read more: coverage by phase of a trip.

Deductibles

What is a deductible in car insurance?

It is the amount you pay toward a covered claim on your own car before your insurer pays the rest. With a $1,000 deductible and $4,000 of damage, the insurer would typically pay $3,000. Read more: how deductibles work.

Do I pay a deductible if the accident was not my fault?

Usually not, if the other driver's insurer pays your claim. If you claim on your own collision coverage first, you may pay your deductible and get it back later if your insurer recovers the money from the at-fault driver's insurer. Read more: deductibles.

Should I choose a $500 or $1,000 deductible?

A higher deductible usually lowers your premium. Choose an amount you could pay right away without borrowing, and compare the real price difference before deciding. Read more: how to choose a deductible.

Does liability insurance have a deductible?

Usually not. Deductibles typically apply to coverage for your own car, such as collision and comprehensive, not to liability claims made by other people.

Costs and saving money

How is car insurance calculated?

Insurers look at factors such as your driving record, age and experience, location, the car, how much you drive, your coverages and deductibles, and in many states your credit-based insurance score. Each insurer weighs these differently. Read more: car insurance calculator.

Why did my car insurance go up?

Common reasons include a recent claim or ticket, higher repair and medical costs in your area, a new driver or car on the policy, a lost discount, or changes in how the insurer prices risk. Read more: why rates go up.

How can I lower my car insurance?

Compare quotes, ask about discounts, raise your deductible if you can afford it, review coverage on older cars, and keep a clean record. Bundling and safe-driving programs may also help. Read more: ways to lower your premium.

What is the best way to compare car insurance?

Get at least three quotes with the same limits, deductibles and coverages so you compare like with like. Then look at price, the insurer's claims reputation and complaint records. Read more: how to compare quotes.

Will my insurance go down if I pay off my car?

Not automatically. Paying off the loan lets you remove coverages the lender required, such as collision, comprehensive or gap, which can lower the premium if you choose to. Read more: full coverage.

Claims and accidents

How do I file a car insurance claim?

Make sure everyone is safe, call the police if needed, exchange information, take photos, and report the claim to your insurer as soon as you can. An adjuster will then review the damage and the policy. Read more: step-by-step claim guide.

How long does a car insurance claim take?

Simple claims may be settled in days or a few weeks. Claims with injuries, disputes over fault or a total loss can take much longer. Many states set deadlines for insurers to respond. Read more: claim timelines.

What should I say to an insurance adjuster?

Stick to the facts, avoid guessing or admitting fault, do not downplay injuries, and keep notes of every call. You can ask for the adjuster's estimate in writing. Read more: what to say to an adjuster.

How long does an accident stay on my insurance?

Often three to five years for pricing purposes, depending on the insurer and state. At-fault accidents usually have more effect than not-at-fault ones. Read more: how long accidents affect rates.

What happens when your car is totaled?

The insurer pays the car's actual cash value, minus your deductible, usually to your lender first if you have a loan. If you owe more than that, gap coverage may pay the difference. Read more: what happens after a total loss.

How much will insurance pay for my totaled car?

Usually the car's actual cash value just before the loss, which is what a similar car would sell for, minus your deductible. You can negotiate if the valuation seems low. Read more: totaled car payouts.

Car loans and gap insurance

What does gap insurance cover?

It covers the difference between what your insurer pays for a totaled or stolen car and what you still owe on the loan or lease. It does not pay for repairs. Read more: gap insurance explained, gap insurance quiz.

Does gap insurance always pay out?

No. It may pay less or nothing if the main claim is denied, the car is not a total loss, the policy caps the payout, or the balance includes excluded amounts such as late fees. Read more: when gap pays.

How long does gap insurance last?

From an insurer, usually as long as you keep it on your policy; from a dealer or lender, usually for the loan term. It stops being useful once you owe less than the car is worth. Read more: gap insurance.

What does upside down on a car loan mean?

It means you owe more on the loan than the car is worth, also called negative equity. It is common in the first years of a loan with a small down payment. Read more: upside-down car loans.

Drivers and special situations

How does car insurance work for new drivers?

Most new drivers who live at home are added to a household policy, which is usually cheaper than a separate one. Prices are higher at first because there is no driving history. Read more: car insurance for first-time drivers.

What is an SR-22?

It is a form your insurer files with the state to prove you carry the required insurance, often after a serious violation or driving without insurance. It is not insurance itself. Read more: SR-22 explained.

Do I need umbrella insurance?

It may make sense if your assets or future income are worth more than your auto and home liability limits. Umbrella policies add extra liability coverage on top of those policies. Read more: umbrella insurance.
